A message record is one individual text message, sent
to or received from a prospect or a lead.
We have zero of them right now.
That is worth understanding because it tells you something
real about where the motion currently is.
But the shape of the object is simple and
it is worth understanding anyway.
Where the conversation object holds a whole back and
forth thread, the message object is the atom.
One row per text.
That granularity is deliberate and it is what makes
the delivery pipeline auditable.
The fields are straightforward.
The body is the actual text that was written.
The direction tells you whether we sent it or
they did, which matters enormously when you are reconstructing
a conversation.
The from number and the number tie the message
to a specific agency phone on our side, and
a specific prospect or lead on theirs.
Then the status field, which is the delivery state,
whether it was queued, sent, delivered, or failed.
And finally, the Telmics Message ID, which is the
reference our provider gives us.
That last field is the important one, because it
lets us go back to the carrier and prove
what actually happened to that specific message.
Why does the business care this much about a
text message log?
Because texting is most of our outbound motion, not
a side channel.
When somebody rings us and misses it, we text
them back.
When we cannot get through on a call, we
text.
When a leave goes quiet, we follow-up by text.
Every one of those steps is a row in
this object, and each of those steps can fail
silently.
A message queued at midnight, a carrier filtering it,
a bad number, a registration that lapsed.
Without this log, we would simply believe we had
followed up, when in fact nothing was ever delivered.
This object is our proof that we did the
thing.
So where does a person actually use this object?
When a lead says they never bought our text,
this is the first place to look, and the
Status field usually settles it immediately.
When we are reconstructing a conversation with a shop,
these rows are evidence And when the agency phone's
record needs auditing, this is where you find out
in practice whether messages are actually landing, because the
failure rate shows up here long before anybody complains.
Zero records today means the texting side is not
running yet.
It also means when it starts, this object is
what makes it trustworthy.
