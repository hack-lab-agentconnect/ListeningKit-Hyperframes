An agency contact record is one page on the
Listening Kit website, 14 of them, at the moment.
One row equals one URL, and that is the
whole mental model.
This object is the content management system.
It is not a blog, it is not a
page builder, and it does not live in a
deployment.
It lives here in the CRM, and the website
reads from it.
That means the person who owns the copy and
the person who owns the pipeline are looking at
the same record, which is unusual and very valuable.
The fields start with identity: the name, the slug,
and the canonical path, which is the definitive address
of the page.
Then the family field, which groups pages that belong
together, like all the tinting pages or all the
career pages.
That grouping is how you change something once and
have it land in many places.
The Status controls whether the page is live or
draft.
Then the SEO title, which is the line that
appears in the search results, and it is edited
here deliberately, rather than left to whatever the page
happened to render.
Finally, the primary call to action and its HRF,
so every page has a clear next step.
Why does it matter that the website renders from
a CRM table?
Because of where the accountability sits.
When a page underperforms, somebody has to change the
copy, change the SEO title, or change the call
to action.
If the content lives in the marketing team, the
CRM never learns anything about it.
If it lives here, then the same record carries
the copy, the search metadata, and the action.
And it sits right next to the campaign records
that are driving traffic to it.
Marketing and sales stop being separate databases and start
being one system.
So where does this object get used day to
day?
When we launch a page, this is the record
that gets written.
When we change an offer, this is where the
wording on the matching page changes.
When we analyze performance, this is where we look
to see whether a weak page is weak because
of its SEO title or call to action.
And the family field is what makes bulk work
possible because updating four tinting pages should be four
edits in the database, not four separate deployments.
14 records today.
This is the object where the search side of
the business and the pipeline side of the business
finally touch.
So, to read a row properly, imagine you have
never seen the website.
The path tells you where the page lives.
The family tells you what kind of page it
is, whether that is a service page, a comparison,
a resource, which means every page of the same
kind, shares a template, and a standard.
The status tells you whether it is published, in
draft, or retired, and that status is the switch
that decides whether it appears at all.
The SEO title is the line that shows up
in a search result, and it is the highest
leverage three seconds of text on the entire page.
The primary call to action is the button you
want somebody to press, and the href is where
it actually goes.
Those last two fields being right or wrong is
the difference between a page that generates a call
and a page somebody visits, nods, and leaves.
Read them in that order and you can tell
somebody exactly what a page is for without opening
it.
