An agency career application is one application we received
against one of our published roles.
We have four at the moment.
This is the object where the careers flow stops
being a website and starts being about actual human
beings who trusted us enough to send us their
CV.
That sounds like a sentimental thing to say about
a database row, but it has a very practical
consequence.
An applicant stored in a shared inbox is a
liability.
An applicant stored as a record with a status
is a pipeline.
The fields are straight forward and complete.
The name, the email, and the phone.
So we can reach them.
The role title which tells you which advert they
came through, and that link is what lets us
compare adverts by quality of applicant.
Then the resume URL, which points at the actual
document they uploaded, so it does not get lost
in an inbox.
Then the cover letter, stored in mine, because the
cover letter usually tells you more about whether somebody
is worth talking to than the CV does.
And the status, which is where the person currently
sits in our hiring process.
Why does it matter that applications get stored here
rather than emailed?
Because everything else in the system has a status,
an owner, and a next action, and hiring is
the only part of the business that runs entirely
on somebody remembering.
When an applicant lives in an inbox, the reply
that was going to go out on Monday does
not go out, and they take another offer.
Putting applications into the CRM means they appear in
the same pipeline view as leads, opportunities, and follow-up
tasks, so nothing quietly falls through.
The status field is the whole game.
So where does this object get used?
Directly, when somebody is reading applications and deciding who
to talk to.
And then less directly, which is more interesting.
The quality of applicants is the honest feedback on
the agency career advert itself.
If one advert gets 40 applications and they are
all strong and another gets four and they are
all off then the problem is the advert, not
the market.
Reading these records backwards is how you learn which
roles to advertise and how to write the next
one.
Four applications today is a very small sample, but
it is the beginning of a real hiring process.
Reading an application well takes about forty five seconds
and almost none of it is spent on the
CV.
Start with the role title and ask whether this
person answered the advert we actually posted or an
advert from three months ago that is still floating
around.
Then the cover signal you get.
Then the cover letter and you are not grading
the writing.
You are looking for one of three things: a
specific reason they want this job, a specific thing
they have actually done, or a question.
Anything else is a template, and a template tells
you the person is mass applying, which is a
fact about them worth knowing before you spend an
hour.
Only after that do you open the resume.
