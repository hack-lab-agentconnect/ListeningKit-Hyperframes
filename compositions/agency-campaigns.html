An agency campaign is a named outbound motion.
We have six of them.
The idea is simple, and it saves an enormous
amount of misery later.
Instead of one enormous undifferentiated pile of 200 prospects,
we group them into campaigns.
So a campaign called window tinting lead gen holds
the tinting shops, the leads that came out of
them, and the scripts written for that motion.
That means when somebody asks how lead gen is
performing, we can answer by looking at one campaign
instead of hand filtering the entire database, and hoping
they did it the same way last time.
The fields tell you two different stories.
The name and the status tell you the business
story, which motion is live, and which are paused.
The campaign type tells you the shape of the
motion Then there are the four technical fields, and
these are the ones people overlook.
There is a URL key, which is a short
unique identifier that gets stamped onto every link we
publish for this campaign.
There is a funnel based URL and a template
based URL, which are where the landing pages actually
live.
And there is a pack directory, which is the
folder of assets and templates that belong to this
specific campaign.
Why do the URL fields matter so much?
Because attribution attaches to the campaign, not to the
prospect.
When we publish a listing page or run an
advert for window tinting, the link that gets clicked
carries this campaign's URL key.
So when a shop brings us back, we can
look at that key and know precisely which motion
produced the conversation.
Without it, every lead just looks like a lead,
and we are arguing about marketing on opinion.
With it, we can kill the campaigns that do
not work.
That is the difference between a sales system and
a guessing contest.
So in practice, where does a person use this
object?
When you open the campaign, you should be able
to answer three questions without leaving the record: Which
prospects are in this motion?
Which leads came out of it?
Which scripts are aimed at it?
And then the fourth question, the real one: Is
it working?
Compare fit scores, compare call connect rates, compare how
far the leads got.
Campaigns exist so that when something is underperforming, we
find out by looking at one screen instead of
arguing.
If you only remember one thing about this object,
remember that it is the unit of measurement for
the whole outbound engine.
So how does a person actually use this object
day to day?
Almost entirely through one field, and that is the
status.
A campaign sits sits in draft while somebody is
still writing the script and building the list.
It goes live when the script, the numbers, and
the compliance checks are all done, and not one
moment before.
And it goes paused when the evidence says stop,
which on outbound is usually a specific and measurable
thing rather than a feeling.
The numbers are in here for a reason.
The URL key is the little identifier that appears
in the link we put in the message, and
the funnel and template base URLs are the two
places that identifier resolves to.
That is how a reply or a booking gets
attributed back to this exact campaign instead of just
to the business.
So the reason this object exists at all in
one sentence is that it lets you switch emotion
off.
Most teams cannot switch anything off.
This one can, and the reason they can is
that the switch lives in a table instead of
in somebody's head.
