An agency prospect is a single real world business
that we might be able to win as a
client.
We have 200 of them in the database right
now.
If you click the object name at the top
of this post, it opens straight into the CRM.
So you can go and look while I talk.
Think of a prospect as the very first card
we deal.
Not a person, not a conversation, not a deal.
Just a business.
The shop itself, sitting in a city, doing a
kind of Auto body shops.
Detailing shops.
Window tinting.
Those are our niches.
Every other object in this whole library hangs off
a prospect, so this is the one to understand
first.
Inside a prospect row, you will find the shop's
name and a slug, which is a clean web
friendly version of that name.
You will find the niche, so we know what
kind of work they do, and the city and
region, so we know where they are.
Then the practical contact stuff.
A phone number we can ring, and an email
address with a confidence score attached, which tells us
how sure we are that the address is genuinely
theirs and not a guess.
There is also an AI fit score out of
100, and a fit reason field which is the
actual sentence explaining why we rated them that way.
So you can always challenge the number, and a
tech stack field which is the list of tools
we detected the shop is already running.
Why does this object matter so much?
Because nothing else in the funnel exists without one
underneath it.
Leads attach to prospects.
Calls get placed to the phone numbers stored here.
Scripts and campaigns are aimed at the niches recorded
here.
This is the spine of the whole database.
Get it wrong and the damage spreads in every
direction.
If somebody merges two shops badly or deletes a
prospect because it looks like a duplicate, you do
not just lose one row you orphan leads that
had no other home, you leave calls pointing at
nothing, and the reporting quietly starts lying to you.
That is exactly why this is the object we
protect most carefully.
Where is it actually used in the day to
day?
We find these shops out in the wild, mostly
from their own public websites and from the directory
listings that the agency listings object tracks.
Once a shop is in here, the fit score
decides who gets worked first, so a high scoring
tinting shop and a good market jumps the queue
over a low scoring one.
Then the phone number stored on the prospect row
is literally the number the dialer calls.
And as deals progress, the Weibel field is how
we mark where they sit.
New. Working. Won. Lost..
When you join the organization and you know nothing
else, open this object first.
It is the map of who we are trying
to reach.
