An agency lead is one specific human being at
a prospect account that we are trying to book
a call with.
We have 18 of them at the moment.
The single most useful thing to hold on to
here is the distinction.
A prospect is the company.
A lead is the person inside that company.
A shopping leads might be one prospect and three
leads because there is an owner, a service manager,
and a body shop manager, and each of them
is a different conversation.
This is also the object where commercial state lands.
Once something is a lead, it has a status
and it can move.
Before that, it is just a row of shops
in a database.
Inside a lead row, you will find the contact
name, so we know exactly who we are talking
to.
You will find the source field which records where
this lead came from, whether that was a campaign,
an inbound inquiry, or something we found ourselves.
Then there are two status fields that people often
confuse.
The status field is the general state of the
relationship.
The qualification status is our own internal judgment of
whether this person is actually worth pursuing, whether they
are in a position to say yes.
And then there is the cold call status, which
tracks specifically how the phone calls have gone.
There is also an outbound message field, which holds
the message we sent them.
Why does a lead matter more than a prospect?
Because this is where we start spending money and
time.
Prospects are cheap.
They are just rows.
A lead is somebody we have decided to ring,
message, and chase.
And critically, the lead is the hinge between a
shop and an actual signed piece of work.
That signed work lives in the agency opportunity object,
which sits on top of a lead.
So if you ever look at a number in
a report and want to know where it came
from, it almost always traces back through a lead.
There are only 18, and some of them will
not convert.
That is fine.
That is what the status field is for.
Where do we use leads day to day?
Two places mainly.
First, the call queue.
When the dialer picks up a job, it is
picking up a lead, and it is reading the
cold call status to know whether that number has
already been run today.
Second, the qualification argument.
Before we spend a consultant hour on a shop,
somebody has to decide if they are real, in
market, and able to pay.
That decision gets recorded on the qualification status.
So the practical summary is this: A prospect is
a maybe.
A lead is a maybe we are actively working.
An opportunity is a yes.
