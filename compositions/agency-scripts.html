A script record is the actual words we say
when we ring somebody, or the actual words we
put in a text message.
We have nine of them.
This is the object people are most surprised to
find in a CRM, because a script feels like
a document, not data.
But the whole point is that it is data.
Once the words live here, they can be handed
to a dialer, versioned, reviewed, linked to the campaign
they belong to, and improved.
A script living in a Google Doc is already
dead.
It drifts, it gets gets forked, and two people
end up saying different things.
There are three fields, and the second one is
the interesting one.
The name identifies the script, something like a cold
call opener for window tinting.
Then script data, which is the script itself, and
the word structured is doing real work in that
sentence.
It is not a blob of prose.
It is the script broken into its parts: the
opening line, the qualifying questions, the objection handling,
the close for the next step.
Because it is structured, software can read it and
pick the right part at the right moment, and
the campaign field links it to the agency campaign
it was written for, so you can tell which
motion it belongs to.
Why insist on keeping scripts in the CRM rather
than in a document?
Consistency If two people sell the same package using
two different stories, the customer experience is incoherent and
we cannot improve either version, because we do not
know which one they heard.
Storing them here means the whole team, and every
automated agent that dials on our behalf, works from
exactly the same source of truth.
It also means means when a script works, everybody
gets the improvement immediately, rather than three weeks later
when somebody mentions it in a meeting.
Where does a script actually get used?
Directly during outreach.
When the dialer places a call, it is reading
from this object.
When we send a follow-up text, the wording comes
from here.
And then there is a second, less obvious use,
which is training.
When a new joiner starts, the fastest way to
understand how the business sells is to read the
nine scripts end to end.
They are short, they are written down, and they
encode the objections we hit, and the answers that
work.
Nine scripts is a tiny object with an outsized
effect on the whole sales floor.
